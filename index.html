01 The shape of the trip

One country,
three moods.

Most 10-day Thailand itineraries try to fit Bangkok, the north and the islands into a week, then spend half of it in transit. This route gives itself 12 days and only two domestic flights — so every stop gets at least three nights.

The order is deliberate: city first, mountains second, beach last. You want the slowest, sunniest part at the end, not a 6 a.m. airport run after five days of temples.

01

Bangkok · 3 nights

The river, the palaces, the food.

Start with the dense, delicious version of Thailand. Temples by boat, Chinatown after dark, and a bed near the Chao Phraya so the first two days need almost no planning.

  • Grand Palace at opening
  • Wat Pho & Wat Arun
  • Yaowarat street-food crawl
02

Chiang Mai · 3 nights

Temples, mountains, khao soi.

The pace drops immediately. Walk the Old City in the morning, eat noodle soup for lunch, and take a songthaew up to Doi Suthep for the late-afternoon light.

  • Old City temple loop
  • Doi Suthep at golden hour
  • Ethical elephant sanctuary
03

Krabi · 5 nights

Longtails, limestone, island days.

Finish on the Andaman with enough time to do nothing. Railay's cliffs, the Four Islands, Phi Phi, and at least two unscheduled beach days.

  • Railay & Phra Nang
  • Four Islands longtail tour
  • Phi Phi day trip

The honest trade-off

Three stops need 12 days. If you only have 10, cut Chiang Mai and fly straight from Bangkok to Krabi. Add the two spare nights to the beach. Trying to keep all three in 10 days is how a holiday becomes a luggage-handling exercise.

02 Day by day

The 12 days,
in order.

Built around the flight times below. Each day has one anchor, a lunch plan and enough empty space to actually enjoy it. Times are local.

01

Bangkok · 3 nights

Land, look up, eat.

Jan 10–13 · Riverside or Old City

Day 01Sun 10 Jan

Land, drop bags, eat well

A travel day with a soft landing. No tour, no alarm.

  • 15:20Delhi → Bangkok. IndiGo non-stop, 4h 15m. Sit on the left for the sunset over the Bay of Bengal.
  • 21:05Land at Suvarnabhumi. eSIM on before passport control. Grab or the airport rail link depending on your hotel.
  • 22:15Check in. Riverside or Banglamphu. Drop the bag, walk to the nearest good street stall.
  • 23:00Late bowl of noodles. Thipsamai if you're near Sam Yot; otherwise whatever has a queue of locals.

Day 02Mon 11 Jan

The royal mile, by boat

The big-ticket temples, done in the right order and before the heat peaks.

  • 08:30Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew. Be at the gate for opening. 500 THB. Covered shoulders and knees; no see-through clothing.
  • 11:30Walk to Wat Pho. 300 THB. The reclining Buddha, then a 30-minute massage at the temple's own school.
  • 15:30Ferry to Wat Arun. 100 THB. Cross from Tha Tien; the prang is best in low light.
  • 18:00Sunset on the east bank. Dinner at Supanniga Eating Room or a river-facing table; Jay Fai needs a reservation or a very early queue.

Dress code: no shorts above the knee, no sleeveless tops, no leggings as trousers. A light scarf is not enough at the Grand Palace.

Day 03Tue 12 Jan

Bangkok beyond the postcard

A green lung, a teak house and the best food street in the city.

  • 08:00Bang Krachao bike loop. Ferry from Khlong Toei, rent a bike for 100 THB, and ride the elevated concrete paths through the jungle.
  • 12:30Lunch at the market. Back across the river; simple rice plates and iced Thai tea.
  • 14:30Jim Thompson House. 200 THB. The teak compound is a calm, air-conditioned antidote to the morning.
  • 18:30Yaowarat food crawl. Nai Ek roll noodle, guay jub, oyster omelette, mango sticky rice. Go hungry, share everything.

Weekend swap: Chatuchak is Saturday–Sunday only. If your dates include a weekend, do the market in the morning and Yaowarat at night.

02

Chiang Mai · 3 nights

Slow mornings, golden temples.

Jan 13–16 · Old City

Day 04Wed 13 Jan

Bangkok → Chiang Mai

A half-day of travel, then northern food and a night market.

  • 09:00Slow breakfast, pack. Leave the big suitcase at the Bangkok hotel if you're returning; otherwise travel light.
  • 13:00Grab to Suvarnabhumi. 45–60 min from Riverside. Two hours before a domestic flight is plenty.
  • 16:15Bangkok → Chiang Mai. Thai AirAsia, 1h 15m. A window seat is worth it for the rice fields and mountains on the approach.
  • 18:30Check in, Night Bazaar. Dinner at The House by Ginger or Ginger Farm Kitchen; wander the Night Bazaar for silk and snacks.

Sleep

Estia Chiang Mai

3 nights · from ₹3,545/night

Day 05Thu 14 Jan

Old City to Doi Suthep

Temples on foot, khao soi for lunch, a mountain for sunset.

  • 08:00Old City temple loop. Wat Chedi Luang (50 THB), Wat Phra Singh, Wat Chiang Man. Walk between them; the square is flat and shaded.
  • 11:30Khao Soi Mae Sai. A Michelin Bib Gourmand bowl for around 70 THB. Go before noon and expect a short queue.
  • 14:00Nimman coffee stop. Air-conditioning, a flat white, and a look at the design shops on Soi 1.
  • 16:00Doi Suthep. Songthaew or private car up the mountain, 30–40 min. 30 THB entry, 306 steps — or take the tram.
  • 19:30Northern dinner. Khantoke set at a cultural restaurant, or street food at Ploen Ruedee Night Market.

Timing: Doi Suthep's courtyard faces east, so late afternoon is for the light on the golden chedi, not the sunset itself. Stay for the city lights coming on below.

Day 06Fri 15 Jan

Elephants, the ethical way

A full day at a no-riding sanctuary, then a massage and an early dinner.

  • 07:30Pickup for Elephant Nature Park. Full-day visit with feeding, walking and river observation. No riding, no bathing shows.
  • 16:30Back in the city. Shower, then a 60-minute Thai massage (300–500 THB).
  • 19:00Dinner at SP Chicken. Wood-fired roast chicken, sticky rice, som tam. Michelin Bib Gourmand, plastic stools, excellent.
  • 21:00Pack for the south. Tomorrow is a travel day; keep a swimsuit and one change of clothes in your carry-on.

Book direct: Elephant Nature Park often sells out in peak season. Reserve at least 2–3 weeks ahead and confirm the pickup time by email.

03

Krabi · 5 nights

Five nights of horizon.

Jan 16–21 · Ao Nang

Day 07Sat 16 Jan

Chiang Mai → Krabi

Coffee, a last bowl, then the two-hour flight south.

  • 08:30Nimman breakfast. One Nimman for coffee and a pastry; pick up a small gift at the design market.
  • 11:00Check out, lunch. Keep it close to the hotel; the airport is only 15 minutes from the Old City.
  • 14:30Chiang Mai airport. Small, quick, but leave the buffer — the domestic terminal gets busy in January.
  • 17:05Chiang Mai → Krabi. Thai AirAsia direct, 2h. Watch the limestone karsts appear as you descend.
  • 20:00Ao Nang check-in. 45-minute transfer. Late seafood on the beach road; the kitchens stay open.

Day 08Sun 17 Jan

Railay, from the water

A longtail to the cliffs, a cave beach, and a steep viewpoint if you're up for it.

  • 08:00Longtail to Railay West. 100–150 THB, 15 minutes from Ao Nang. Boats leave when full.
  • 09:00Phra Nang Cave Beach. Swim, watch the climbers, and keep an eye on your bag — the monkeys are bold.
  • 11:30Railay viewpoint & lagoon. A steep 45-minute scramble. Trainers, not flip-flops; the lagoon is not a swim.
  • 14:00Lunch on Railay. Then back to Ao Nang for pool time and a sunset walk.
  • 18:30Seafood grill. Pick your fish, agree the price by weight, and eat with your feet in the sand.

Carry cash

Day 09Mon 18 Jan

The Four Islands

The classic Krabi boat day: caves, sandbars and a lagoon the colour of glass.

  • 08:00Longtail tour. Chicken Island, Tup Island, Poda Island and Phra Nang. Around 1,200 THB with lunch; park fees extra.
  • 12:00Lunch on Poda. Simple fried rice and fruit, eaten on the sand. Bring water and a dry bag.
  • 15:30Back to Ao Nang. Shower, nap, then a night-market dinner at Ao Nang Landmark.
  • 19:30Massage on the beach road. 300–400 THB for an hour. Book the 8 p.m. slot before dinner.

Which side of the boat: sit on the left for the best light on the limestone cliffs in the morning; bring a rash vest if you burn easily.

Day 10Tue 19 Jan

Phi Phi, early

Maya Bay before the crowds, snorkelling in the middle, back for sunset.

  • 07:30Speedboat to Phi Phi. Maya Bay, Phi Phi Don and two snorkel stops. 1,500–2,000 THB plus the 400 THB national-park fee.
  • 11:00Maya Bay. The beach is stunning; the permit system limits numbers, but it still fills up. Go with an operator that arrives early.
  • 13:00Lunch on Phi Phi Don. Then a short snorkel stop on the way back.
  • 16:30Back in Ao Nang. Sunset at Nopparat Thara, dinner at The Last Fisherman Bar.

Alternative: swap Phi Phi for a Hong Islands kayak day if you want fewer people and more mangroves. Both are weather-dependent; ask your hotel to check the marine forecast.

Nothing day

The most important day of the itinerary. Protect it.

  • 09:00Breakfast, no plan. Pool, book, beach. Do not book a tour.
  • 13:00Optional: Hong Islands kayak. If you have energy, the lagoon and mangroves are worth a half-day.
  • 17:30Nopparat Thara sunset. Walk the length of the beach; it's quieter than Ao Nang proper.
  • 19:30Final dinner. Crab curry, grilled prawns, roti. Pack before bed.

A midday flight, a tight-but-legal connection, and Delhi by evening.

05 What to eat

Eat like
you mean it.

Thai food in Thailand is not one cuisine. The north is about noodles, herbs and grilled meat; the south is coconut, turmeric and seafood; Bangkok is everything, all at once, at 11 p.m.

These are the dishes worth planning a day around — and the places where the version is reliably good.

The one dish

Khao soi

Coconut curry broth, soft egg noodles, crunchy fried noodles on top, pickled mustard greens and lime on the side. Chiang Mai's signature bowl costs less than a coffee in Delhi and ruins every Thai restaurant at home.

Where: Khao Soi Mae Sai, near Chang Phuak. Go before 2 p.m.; it closes when the pot runs out.

60–90 THB

01

Pad thai

The benchmark is Thipsamai in Bangkok: tamarind, prawns, a thin omelette. Ask for less sugar and more lime.

02

Mango sticky rice

Coconut-soaked glutinous rice, ripe nam dok mai mango, salted coconut cream. Peak mango is March–June; January is still good.

03

Som tam

Green papaya, lime, fish sauce, palm sugar, chilies, peanuts. Say mai sai prik if you don't do heat — and mean it.

04

Massaman curry

Muslim-Thai curry with beef, potato, peanuts and warm spices. Best with roti, ideally from a street cart in the south.

05

Andaman seafood

Grilled prawns, crab curry, lime-chilli squid. Pick your fish at Ao Nang's night market and agree the price by weight.

06

Sai ua

Northern Thai herb sausage, grilled over charcoal and served with sticky rice. The best snack between temples in Chiang Mai.

07 Good to know

The boring bits,
sorted.

The rules changed in September 2026, and the details matter. Everything below is for an Indian passport holder arriving on a tourist trip.

01 Visa & entry

Indian ordinary passports get a 30-day visa exemption for tourism, effective 15 September 2026. You need a passport valid for at least six months, proof of onward travel, and enough funds for your stay. Immigration may ask for a hotel booking.

Complete the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) within 72 hours before arrival and keep the QR code. It is free. Don't pay a third-party site to fill it for you.

Royal Thai Embassy, New Delhi ↗Official TDAC portal ↗

02 When to go

November to February is the sweet spot: the Andaman is dry, Bangkok is hot but bearable, and Chiang Mai is cool in the mornings (15–20°C) and warm by afternoon. January is peak season, so book flights and hotels early.

March–April is hot and smoky in the north. May–October is monsoon season on the Andaman; the Gulf islands (Koh Samui) run on a different calendar.

03 Money & tipping

The baht is the only currency you need. At the time of writing, ₹1 ≈ 0.38 THB; check the live rate before you go. Thai ATMs charge a 220 THB foreign-card fee, so withdraw larger amounts less often.

Always choose to be charged in THB, not your home currency. Tipping is not expected at street stalls; 20–50 THB for a massage or 5–10% at a sit-down restaurant is appreciated.

04 Getting around

Bangkok: BTS Skytrain and MRT for anything near the river or Sukhumvit; Grab for everything else. The Chao Phraya Express Boat is both transport and sightseeing.

Chiang Mai: red songthaews cost 30–50 THB for a shared ride inside the city; agree the price before getting in. Krabi: longtails between Ao Nang and Railay are 100–150 THB; airport transfers are 600–800 THB private.

05 Temples & etiquette

Cover shoulders and knees at every temple; remove shoes before entering the main hall. Don't point your feet at a Buddha image, and don't touch monks if you're a woman. A light linen shirt and loose trousers are more comfortable than a sarong in the heat.

The Grand Palace is strict: no leggings, no sleeveless tops, no sheer fabrics. Shorts must cover the knee. There is a free borrowing desk, but sizes run out.

06 Scams & safety

Thailand is generally safe for tourists; traffic is the real risk. The classic scam is a tuk-tuk driver saying the Grand Palace is “closed today” and offering a tour instead. It isn't closed. Walk to the official gate.

Agree taxi fares or use the meter, photograph jet-ski damage before renting, and don't hand over your passport as a deposit. Emergency: 191; Tourist Police: 1155.

07 Connectivity & packing

An eSIM with 15–20GB for 10 days costs ₹600–900 and activates on landing. Coverage is good in cities and on the islands; download offline maps anyway.

Pack light layers for the north, reef-safe sunscreen, mosquito repellent, a dry bag, trainers for the Railay viewpoint, and a universal adapter (Thailand uses A/B/C plugs, 230V).

08 Booking order

Now: international flight and the two domestic legs. 3–5 months out: hotels on free cancellation. 3–4 weeks out: Elephant Nature Park and any Phi Phi tour. 72 hours out: TDAC and eSIM.

If you're travelling over Christmas or New Year, add 30–50% to every number on this page and book six months ahead.
